How This Works

The rules, before the rates.

Most white-label arrangements fall apart for the same reason. So the boundaries come first, in writing, before anyone talks about money.

01

We never contact your client. Ever.

Not during the shoot, not after, not a year later. No cards, no follow-up, no "just checking in." If they ask who we are, we are your production team. This is the whole deal and we treat it that way.

02

Everything arrives unbranded.

No watermark, no logo, no metadata with our name in it. Put your badge on it and send it as your own work. That is what you are paying for.

03

You set the retail price.

We quote you trade. What you charge your client is between you and your client, and we never need to know it.

04

You are our client, not them.

You brief us, you approve the cut, you get the invoice. We do not manage your account for you and we do not want to.

05

Raw files back in 72 hours.

You have already promised your client a date. If you have your own editor, you are cutting two days after we wrap.

What You Are
Actually Worried About

What stops you going around me?

A signed non-solicit before the first shoot, and the fact that it would be the dumbest trade we could make. One agency sending us work all year is worth more than one client we stole once. We will put it in writing before you send us anything.

What if your work is not up to our standard?

Book one job and find out on something low-stakes before you put us in front of a client that matters. If the first delivery is not what you would put your name on, you do not pay for it.

We only need this occasionally.

That is the point. There is no retainer, no minimum, and no volume commitment. Use us twice a year or twice a month. Nothing changes either way.

Our client is outside Tampa Bay.

Tampa Bay, Sarasota, and Orlando are in the rate as published. Past about ninety minutes we add mileage, quoted before you book so there is no surprise on the invoice. Much further than that and we will tell you honestly that you want someone local.

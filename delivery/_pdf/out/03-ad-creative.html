Client Delivery System

Ad Creative

Angle bank, two video cuts, one static

Internal — not for client distribution creativelygrow.com

Owner: Claude drafts, Devon cuts. Everything here is built from the shoot — no stock, no templates-with-their-logo-on.

Two video ads and one static, all cut from the same day's footage. Channel is decided in 06-traffic.md; this doc is the creative regardless of where it runs.

The principle

The creative is the targeting. Modern ad platforms find the audience — your job is to make something that self-selects the right person in the first two seconds. A tight audience with weak creative loses to broad targeting with creative that speaks to exactly one person's problem.

Practical consequence: don't build six audiences. Build six angles. Let the platform sort it out.

Step 1 — The angle bank [do this before writing anything]

Pull straight from intake §2.1 and §2.4 — the owner's verbatim words and the customer fear. Do not invent angles from imagination.

Write 4–6. Each one is a single sentence naming one specific person's problem.

#AngleSourceFor
1The fear — the thing they worry about before callingIntake §2.4Cold
2The differentiator — what they do that others skipIntake §2.1Cold
3The price question — what it actually costs, said plainlyIntake §2.1Consideration
4The timeline — how long, how disruptedIntake §2.1Consideration
5The proof — the finished work speaking for itselfShoot footageRetarget
6The unwanted job, inverted — "we're the people for X, not Y"Intake §2.1Cold

Good angle: "Your garage floor is peeling because whoever did it skipped the grind." Bad angle: "Quality flooring solutions for discerning homeowners."

The test: could a competitor put their logo on it? If yes, it isn't an angle.

Step 2 — Video Ad A · Problem / Agitate

9:16 · 15–30s · cold audience

The hook does all the work. If the first two seconds don't land, nothing else in the ad matters.

BeatTimeContent
Hook0–2sThe problem, visually. The damage, the failure, the mess
Name it2–6sOne line of text or VO stating the problem plainly
Turn6–10sCut to the process — the money shot
Proof10–20sThe state change. Before → after
Ask20–30sOwner to camera or text card. One CTA

Hook library (pick by trade, all cut from Part 3 of the treatment)

  • The failure, close and unflattering — peeling, sagging, cracked, filthy
  • Hand pointing at the problem
  • The unflattering before frame, held one beat too long
  • Text-on-screen question: "How long since anyone looked at this?"
  • The money shot cold-opened with no setup

Text overlay rules

  • Big enough to read at arm's length on a phone
  • Assume sound is off — the ad must work silent
  • One idea per card, never two

Step 3 — Video Ad B · Proof / Authority

9:16 · 15–30s · warm and retargeting

This is the brand story film, cut down. Same footage, different job: the person already knows they have the problem. Now they're deciding who to trust.

BeatTimeContent
Face0–3sOwner, talking, in their space. A real person opens it
Credential3–8sYears, crew, what they do — plainly, no boasting
The line8–18sTheir best verbatim line from the interview (usually the answer to Q7)
Work18–26sProcess and finished result under the line
Ask26–30sOne CTA

This is where the film pays for itself. Nobody else in the local market has a real owner on camera saying something true.

Step 4 — Static Ad

1:1 and 4:5 · both sizes, same design

One claim. One image. One CTA. Static is for the person who's seen the video and needs a reason to act now, or for a Google display placement where video won't run.

  • Image: the strongest single frame from the shoot — usually the after, or a matched before/after split
  • Headline: 5–8 words, the angle
  • Sub: one line, the specific
  • CTA: the same words as the landing page button — never a different phrase
  • Logo: small, corner, not the point

Do not put five bullet points, a phone number, a QR code, and a starburst on it. The image does the work.

Step 5 — Copy

Same structure whichever platform. Write one set per angle.

Primary text

[Line 1 — the problem, in their words. Must stand alone; the rest is truncated on mobile.]

[Line 2–3 — what you do about it, specifically.]

[Line 4 — the honest range or timeline.]

[CTA line.]

Headline: under 40 characters, the angle not the brand Description: the specific — service area, timeline, or range

If Google instead of Meta (client declined video ads — see 06-traffic.md)

  • 15 headlines, 30 chars each — mix service + city + differentiator + price
  • 4 descriptions, 90 chars each
  • Sitelinks pointing at the landing page sections
  • Callouts: licensed, insured, years, warranty — whatever's actually true

Step 6 — What to test

Test one variable at a time, in this order. Most accounts test the wrong thing first.

  1. Hook (first 2s) — the highest-leverage variable by a wide margin
  2. Angle — which problem statement pulls
  3. Format — video A vs B vs static
  4. Copy — primary text variants
  5. Everything else — rarely worth the traffic

Don't test: button colours, tiny copy tweaks, audience slicing. There isn't enough volume at a local budget to read those, and pretending otherwise burns the month.

Rules

  1. Never use footage from another client's job. Not once, not "just as a placeholder." One

discovery ends the relationship and deserves to.

  1. No invented numbers. No "97% satisfaction," no made-up job counts, no fake review screenshots.

If the real number isn't known, describe the mechanism.

  1. No fake urgency. No countdowns that reset, no "3 spots left" that's always 3.
  2. Before/after must be the same job, same position. A before from one job and an after from

another is a fabricated result.

  1. Claims must be verifiable. Licensed, insured, warrantied — only if intake confirmed it.
  2. The CTA on the ad matches the CTA on the page. A mismatch is the most common reason a good ad

converts badly.

  1. Silent-first. Most of the audience never turns sound on.

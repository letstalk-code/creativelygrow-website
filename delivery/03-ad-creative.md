# Phase 4 — Ad Creative

**Owner: Claude drafts, Devon cuts.** Everything here is built from the shoot — no stock, no
templates-with-their-logo-on.

Two video ads and one static, all cut from the same day's footage. Channel is decided in
`06-traffic.md`; this doc is the creative regardless of where it runs.

---

## The principle

**The creative is the targeting.** Modern ad platforms find the audience — your job is to make
something that self-selects the right person in the first two seconds. A tight audience with weak
creative loses to broad targeting with creative that speaks to exactly one person's problem.

Practical consequence: **don't build six audiences. Build six angles.** Let the platform sort it out.

---

## Step 1 — The angle bank `[do this before writing anything]`

Pull straight from intake §2.1 and §2.4 — the owner's verbatim words and the customer fear. Do not
invent angles from imagination.

Write 4–6. Each one is a single sentence naming **one specific person's problem.**

| # | Angle | Source | For |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *The fear* — the thing they worry about before calling | Intake §2.4 | Cold |
| 2 | *The differentiator* — what they do that others skip | Intake §2.1 | Cold |
| 3 | *The price question* — what it actually costs, said plainly | Intake §2.1 | Consideration |
| 4 | *The timeline* — how long, how disrupted | Intake §2.1 | Consideration |
| 5 | *The proof* — the finished work speaking for itself | Shoot footage | Retarget |
| 6 | *The unwanted job, inverted* — "we're the people for X, not Y" | Intake §2.1 | Cold |

**Good angle:** "Your garage floor is peeling because whoever did it skipped the grind."
**Bad angle:** "Quality flooring solutions for discerning homeowners."

The test: could a competitor put their logo on it? If yes, it isn't an angle.

## Step 2 — Video Ad A · Problem / Agitate

**9:16 · 15–30s · cold audience**

The hook does all the work. If the first two seconds don't land, nothing else in the ad matters.

| Beat | Time | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–2s | The problem, visually. The damage, the failure, the mess |
| Name it | 2–6s | One line of text or VO stating the problem plainly |
| Turn | 6–10s | Cut to the process — the money shot |
| Proof | 10–20s | The state change. Before → after |
| Ask | 20–30s | Owner to camera or text card. One CTA |

**Hook library** *(pick by trade, all cut from Part 3 of the treatment)*
- The failure, close and unflattering — peeling, sagging, cracked, filthy
- Hand pointing at the problem
- The unflattering before frame, held one beat too long
- Text-on-screen question: *"How long since anyone looked at this?"*
- The money shot cold-opened with no setup

**Text overlay rules**
- Big enough to read at arm's length on a phone
- Assume sound is off — the ad must work silent
- One idea per card, never two

## Step 3 — Video Ad B · Proof / Authority

**9:16 · 15–30s · warm and retargeting**

Cut from the same shoot as Ad A — the owner-on-camera offer plus the crew b-roll, not a separate
piece. If a longer brand story cut exists, pull from that too. Different job from Ad A: the person
already knows they have the problem. Now they're deciding who to trust.

| Beat | Time | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Face | 0–3s | Owner, talking, in their space. A real person opens it |
| Credential | 3–8s | Years, crew, what they do — plainly, no boasting |
| The line | 8–18s | Their best verbatim line from the interview *(usually the answer to Q7)* |
| Work | 18–26s | Process and finished result under the line |
| Ask | 26–30s | One CTA |

**This is where the film pays for itself.** Nobody else in the local market has a real owner on
camera saying something true.

## Step 4 — Static Ad

**1:1 and 4:5 · both sizes, same design**

One claim. One image. One CTA. Static is for the person who's seen the video and needs a reason to act
now, or for a Google display placement where video won't run.

- **Image:** the strongest single frame from the shoot — a crew or work still by default, a
  matched before/after only if the visit happened to catch both
- **Headline:** 5–8 words, the angle
- **Sub:** one line, the specific
- **CTA:** the same words as the landing page button — never a different phrase
- **Logo:** small, corner, not the point

**Do not** put five bullet points, a phone number, a QR code, and a starburst on it. The image does
the work.

---

## Step 5 — Copy

Same structure whichever platform. Write one set per angle.

**Primary text**
```
[Line 1 — the problem, in their words. Must stand alone; the rest is truncated on mobile.]

[Line 2–3 — what you do about it, specifically.]

[Line 4 — the honest range or timeline.]

[CTA line.]
```

**Headline:** under 40 characters, the angle not the brand
**Description:** the specific — service area, timeline, or range

**If Google instead of Meta** *(client declined video ads — see `06-traffic.md`)*
- 15 headlines, 30 chars each — mix service + city + differentiator + price
- 4 descriptions, 90 chars each
- Sitelinks pointing at the landing page sections
- Callouts: licensed, insured, years, warranty — whatever's actually true

---

## Step 6 — What to test

Test **one variable at a time**, in this order. Most accounts test the wrong thing first.

1. **Hook** (first 2s) — the highest-leverage variable by a wide margin
2. **Angle** — which problem statement pulls
3. **Format** — video A vs B vs static
4. **Copy** — primary text variants
5. **Everything else** — rarely worth the traffic

**Don't test:** button colours, tiny copy tweaks, audience slicing. There isn't enough volume at a
local budget to read those, and pretending otherwise burns the month.

---

## Rules

1. **Never use footage from another client's job.** Not once, not "just as a placeholder." One
   discovery ends the relationship and deserves to.
2. **No invented numbers.** No "97% satisfaction," no made-up job counts, no fake review screenshots.
   If the real number isn't known, describe the mechanism.
3. **No fake urgency.** No countdowns that reset, no "3 spots left" that's always 3.
4. **Before/after must be the same job**, same position. A before from one job and an after from
   another is a fabricated result.
5. **Claims must be verifiable.** Licensed, insured, warrantied — only if intake confirmed it.
6. **The CTA on the ad matches the CTA on the page.** A mismatch is the most common reason a good ad
   converts badly.
7. **Silent-first.** Most of the audience never turns sound on.

# Phase 5 — Reporting

**Owner: Claude compiles, Devon delivers.**

`/how-it-works` already promises this to every client: *"Every pilot tracks the moments between
'someone found you' and 'someone came back,' so improvement is measured, not assumed."* This doc is
how that promise gets kept.

---

## The six measures

The same six, every client, every report. Don't invent new ones per client — consistency is what
makes Day 90 comparable to Day 0.

| # | Measure | Source | Why it's on the list |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Source of inquiry | GHL lead tagging (`06-traffic.md`) | Where the money's actually working |
| 2 | Speed to first response | GHL timestamps: inquiry → first outbound touch | The single biggest lever on close rate |
| 3 | Missed calls recovered | Missed-call text-back log | Proof the cheapest fix is paying off |
| 4 | Inquiries → bookings | GHL opportunity pipeline | The number that actually matters to the client |
| 5 | Follow-up status | GHL: any open inquiry >24h untouched | Catches leads quietly dying in an inbox |
| 6 | Reviews and repeat signals | GBP review count/rating, repeat-customer tag | Prominence input + retention signal |

---

## Baseline — captured Day 0, never estimated after

Pull from `01b-seo-audit.md` the moment intake closes, before anything is touched:

- GBP review count and rating (screenshot)
- SEO audit health score
- Indexed page count
- Current rankings for the top 3 service + city terms
- Whatever review count / rating the client stated in intake §2.3, cross-checked against the screenshot

**Without this, Day 90 is an opinion.** With it, it's "reviews went 4 → 19, three service pages now
rank page one, response time went from hours to under five minutes." Numbers, not a feeling.

---

## Weekly (internal, not client-facing)

Five minutes, checked against the kill/promote rules in `06-traffic.md`:

- Any lead sitting untouched >24h? Flag it to the client same day — this one's urgent, not a report item
- Spend vs. the budget floor — on track to reach a verdict, or is the pace wrong
- Any ad clearly underperforming the set

---

## Monthly — the client-facing report

**One page. A contractor will not read three.** Structure:

1. **The headline number** — one sentence. "You booked 11 jobs from 34 inquiries this month."
2. **The six measures**, this month vs. last month vs. baseline, in a simple table
3. **What we changed** — 2–3 bullets, plain language, no jargon
4. **What's next** — 1–2 bullets, what you're doing in response to the data

**Never include:** raw ad platform screenshots, jargon like CTR/CPM/ROAS without translation, more
than one page, anything that reads as padding to look busy.

If a number is bad, say so and say what you're doing about it. A client who gets a bad month explained
honestly trusts the next report. A client who only ever sees good news stops trusting any of them.

---

## Day 30 — first real read

- Cost per qualified lead, by channel
- Cost per booked job (the number that actually decides if this is working)
- Is the channel decision (`06-traffic.md`) still the right one, now that there's real data?
- Any structural fix still outstanding — a form that's leaking, a page that's slow, calls still
  going unanswered

---

## Day 90 — the pilot review

The moment `/how-it-works` describes: *"We review inquiry quality and real business feedback, then
improve conversion, response, reputation, and repeat business."*

Bring the arithmetic, not a vibe:

- Full baseline-to-Day-90 comparison, all six measures
- Cost per booked job vs. the client's own average job value from intake §2.1 — does the math clear?
- Recommendation: **continue as-is, adjust the structure, or stop.** State it plainly. If it doesn't
  clear for the client's numbers, say so — that's the promise already made on the homepage: *"If it
  doesn't clear for your numbers, we'll say so."*

---

## Rules

1. **Same six measures for every client.** Never customize the list — customize the read.
2. **Baseline before touching anything.** No exceptions, no "we'll estimate it later."
3. **One page for the client.** Detail belongs in the working notes, not the deliverable.
4. **Bad numbers get said plainly.** Spin costs more trust than it protects.
5. **Day 90 has to actually conclude something.** Continue, adjust, or stop — not "let's keep watching."

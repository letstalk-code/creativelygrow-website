## The rule that governs everything here

**A brand story film is proof, not advertising.** The customer is buying a promise about work that
doesn't exist yet, from someone they met once. The film's only job is to make that promise credible.

So: real crew, real work, real voice. No stock. No "we're passionate about quality." If a line could
appear on any competitor's site, cut it.

## Part 2 — Structure

Default 90 seconds. Five beats.

| Beat | Sec | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The hook** | 0–8 | The most visually arresting thing on the job. Not a logo. Not a drone shot of the town. The work. |
| **2. Who they are** | 8–25 | Owner on camera, in their space. Name, trade, how long. Plain. |
| **3. The problem they solve** | 25–50 | The fear, named and answered. Cut to work that proves it. |
| **4. Proof** | 50–75 | Process, crew, craft. The unglamorous competence. This is the part that sells. |
| **5. The close** | 75–90 | Owner, direct to camera, one sentence. Then logo + phone. |

**Cutdowns from the same shoot** — plan these on the day, don't reframe later:
- **9:16 × 3** (15–30s) for ads — hook must land in 2 seconds
- **6s bumper** — one shot, one line, logo

## Part 4 — Interview questions

Ask these. Let them ramble. The usable line is almost always the third sentence of an answer, after
they stop performing.

1. Tell me what you do, like you're telling a neighbor.
2. How'd you get into this?
3. What's the part of the job most people don't see?
4. What do customers worry about before they call you?
5. What do you do differently from the guy down the road?
6. Tell me about a job you're proud of.
7. What goes wrong on a bad job — and what do you do about it?
8. Why should someone pick you?

**Q7 is the most valuable question on this list.** Everyone answers 5 and 8 with a slogan. Nobody
rehearses 7, so that's where the credible answer lives.

**Never feed them lines.** If they say something great, ask them to say it again — don't say it for them.

---

## Rules

1. **Never script the owner.** Ask questions, cut the answers.
2. **Over-shoot b-roll.** You'll never wish you had less. Every ad for the next year comes from this day.
3. **Shoot vertical on the day.** Reframed 16:9 always looks reframed.
4. **No stock footage. Ever.** The entire point is that this is real.
5. **If the site is a mess, reschedule or clean it.** You cannot fix it in the edit, and the client
   will blame the film.

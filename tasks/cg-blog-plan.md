## 2. The thesis, in one paragraph

Labif ranks because it writes venue guides nobody else can write — first-hand authority is the
moat. Creatively Grow's version has to work differently, because the current verticals (epoxy
flooring, pool cage / screen enclosures) have **no organic search demand at the vertical's own
name**. So we publish at the altitude where demand exists — **contractor and remodeling growth
content** — and use epoxy floors and pool cages as the worked examples throughout. A contractor
searching "contractor SEO" finds a post that answers the general question and then shows, in
detail, what it looked like for an epoxy company. That's the moat: everyone else writes these
posts in the abstract; we can show a real demo build and a real customer path.

Two payoffs: it's **inbound-first prospecting** (the reader of "marketing for contractors" *is*
the buyer), and the epoxy/pool-cage examples double as sales collateral for the outbound sprint
already running in `todo.md`.

**Honest timing caveat:** organic content is a 6–12 month asset. If the near-term need is booked
epoxy/screen clients *this quarter*, the instruments for that are the `epoxy.html` /
`pool-cage.html` demos, GBP, and the outbound sprint — not this blog. Build the blog as the
compounding asset underneath those, not as the thing expected to produce August revenue.

## 3. HARD RULES — do not break these

1. **No "agency/service + Tampa" blog posts.** Data above proves the volume isn't there and the
   head term is KD 39. Local agency proximity is a **GBP play**. Do not write a post targeting
   `marketing agency tampa`, `video production tampa`, or any sibling.
2. **Never video-led.** Video is the entry product. The blog's subject is the client's growth
   problem; video appears as one mechanism inside the system, never the headline thesis.
3. **Only claim first-hand results for businesses we have actually worked with** — the named
   brand-film clients, and the demo builds (`epoxy.html`, `pool-cage.html`) which must be
   described honestly as demos, never as delivered client engagements. Never invent a "we ran
   this for a client" claim.
4. **Never fabricate a metric.** No made-up percentages, ROI figures, or case-study numbers. If we
   don't have the real number, describe the *mechanism* instead. A fabricated stat is the single
   fastest way to destroy an agency's credibility.
5. **No client name, number, or result is published without Devon's explicit OK.** Real client data
   requires permission — this is a harder rule than Labif's because these are B2B relationships.
6. **Every post needs:** a 40–60 word quotable opening answer, scannable H2s, concrete specifics,
   a 3–5 question FAQ block (powers FAQPage schema), the Creatively Grow byline, internal links
   (Section 5), and a strategy-call CTA. No post ships without the FAQ block.
7. **One post at a time, newest-first.** Complete and verified before the next.

- `/contact` or `/book` as the single CTA destination (schema already references `/book`)
- A `/blog` index
- Ideally 2–3 service pages so trade posts have a commercial destination

(the 90-day pilot is the offer), and the CTA. Reciprocal links added from older posts as the
cluster grows.
